What you are doing here (read this first)

These clips are sampled from the library that AI agents use to put motion into games. Someone making a game asks an agent for, say, "a character running away", and the agent picks clips from this library.

How an agent chooses a clip: by its words (its name, tags and description) and its numbers (how fast it travels, whether it loops, how long it is). It never watches the clip play: at most it reads a short summary a computer makes of the movement ("walks", "in the air") or looks at a few still frames. So a clip whose words don't match its motion misleads every agent that picks it.

What the sensors already check: feet sliding on the ground, sinking into the floor, sudden jumps between frames, arms or legs passing through the body. What they can't: whether a clip is what its words say, and whether it looks natural and alive. That is your part. Each card's PASS, WARN or FAIL is the sensors' result; a WARN, or a frame marked red on its preview sheet, is where to look closely. It doesn't decide for you.

For each card:

  1. Press Play and watch it (its gallery link opens it bigger, slower and from any side). The test character's left side is blue, its back darker and its nose red, so you can tell which way it faces and whether it walks forward or backwards.
  2. Read What it should show: what an agent reads about it.
  3. Approve if a game developer asking for exactly this would be happy with it.
  4. Flag if it looks broken, doesn't match its words (called a run but it is a walk, an idle that is a frozen pose, a jump that never leaves the ground), or no game could use it. Say why in a few words. An idle needn't travel, but its body should move.

Notes: one starting "task:" proposes something to fix or build; one starting "threshold:" says a sensor's limit looks wrong (it let a flaw through, or stopped a good clip). A clip someone sent in is adopted, archived or rejected the same way.

When you are done: press Export review at the bottom and send the file back to whoever asked you for this review (they apply it with one command). Playing a clip or reading a card decides nothing; only Approve and Flag do. Your choices are kept in this browser, and they count once the file is sent.
